Unlucky Numbers in Chinese Culture

By 270270 EditorialUpdated {TODAY}6 min read

One digit can quietly sink a sale. Here are the numbers Chinese speakers avoid, why they avoid them, and the surprising exceptions.

The big one: 4 (四 sì ≈ 死 sǐ, "death")

Fear of 4 (tetraphobia) is the most widespread number superstition in East Asia. Many buildings in Chinese communities skip the 4th, 14th and 24th floors. Hospitals avoid room 4. Phone numbers and plates containing 4 sell cheaply, and gifts never come in fours.

Insults hiding in numbers

  • 250 (二百五) means "idiot". Never give ¥250 or price a gift at 250.
  • 38 (三八) is a derogatory word for a gossipy woman.
  • 13 (十三点) is Shanghainese slang for someone silly. Western 13-avoidance adds to it.

When 4 is actually fine

Inside some phrases, 4 reads as 是 (shì, "yes") or 世 (shì, "lifetime"): 1314 (forever), 3344 (life after life), 94 ("exactly"), 2014 ("love you for a lifetime"). In Cantonese, 54 (唔死) means "won't die".

Other traps

  • 3 in gifts: 散 (sàn) means "to scatter or break up". Avoid it in wedding gifts.
  • Ghost Month: the 7th lunar month is traditionally avoided for weddings, moving and big launches. See the Date Finder.
  • Clocks as gifts: 送钟 sounds like 送终, "attending a funeral". It's not a number, but it's a classic mistake.
