Lucky Pricing: How to Price for Chinese Customers

By 270270 EditorialUpdated {TODAY}7 min read

Western retail loves .99. Chinese-speaking customers are more likely to notice an 8. Here's how to build a price list that feels prosperous, not unlucky.

Rule 1: end on 8 (or 6, or 9)

8 (发, prosper) is the strongest ending. 6 (顺, smooth) and 9 (久, lasting) are good alternatives. Menus in Chinese restaurants commonly show prices like $58, $68 and $88.

Rule 2: build a lucky price ladder

Good tiers read like a story: 68 → 88 → 168 → 288 → 888. Bundles at 168, 518 or 1688 signal generosity and good wishes. Keep it consistent across currencies (C$, S$, HK$, RM) where your pricing allows.

Rule 3: avoid the traps

  • Any 4, especially as the last digit (44, 14, 24, 74).
  • 250 and anything that reads as an insult (二百五).
  • 748 (去死吧) in promo codes, SKUs or phone numbers.
  • Odd amounts for gifts and red envelopes: gifts are given in even amounts (except funerals).

Rule 4: time campaigns to number festivals

  • Lunar New Year (Jan/Feb): red packaging, 8-ending bundles, 🧧 gift cards.
  • 5.20 / 5.21: couples' promotions (我爱你 / 我愿意).
  • 6.18 and 11.11 (Singles' Day): the largest online sales events in the Chinese-speaking world.
  • Qixi (lunar 7/7, Chinese Valentine's Day): jewellery, flowers, dining.
