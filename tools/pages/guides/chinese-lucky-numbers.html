1. Why numbers carry luck

Chinese is rich in homophones: many words share the same syllable and differ only by tone, or not at all. When a number is spoken, listeners hear the words it sounds like. 8 (bā) sounds close to 发 (fā, "prosper"), so it's welcome everywhere. 4 (sì) sounds almost identical to 死 (sǐ, "death"), so it's avoided. This is homophonic numerology, and it shapes real decisions: prices, phone numbers, addresses, dates and gift amounts.

3. Famous combinations

  • 168: 一路发, "prosper all the way". A classic for prices and company names.
  • 518: 我要发, "I will prosper".
  • 888 and 8888: prosperity multiplied.
  • 66: 六六大顺, "everything goes smoothly".
  • 520: 我爱你, "I love you". 20 May is China's internet Valentine's Day.
  • 1314: 一生一世, "forever". Together they make 5201314.
  • 99: 久久, "forever and ever". 999 roses mean eternal love.
  • 28: 易发, "easy prosperity", especially in Cantonese.
💡 Context changes everything. 4 is unlucky alone, yet 3344 (生生世世, "life after life") and 94 (就是, "exactly") are positive, because 4 there sounds like 世 or 是.

4. Mandarin vs Cantonese

Most meanings are shared, but a few flip. In Cantonese, 5 (ng) sounds like 唔 "not". 58 is "I prosper" in Mandarin but "won't prosper" in Cantonese, while 54 becomes "won't die", a lucky reading. 3 (saam) sounds like 生 "life", which makes it more positive in Hong Kong. 7 has a vulgar Cantonese homophone, so brands in Hong Kong use it carefully.
